# Project Synopsis — RD-OS

## What it is

RD-OS (Rapid Deploy Operating System) is an AI-assisted operational workspace built for small
businesses — the kind that run on one or two people who hold the whole operation in their heads.

## The problem it addresses

Small businesses rarely lose information outright. They lose track of *where they put it*.

A catering enquiry arrives by Instagram DM. The requirements get discussed on a call and written
in a notebook. The quote goes out by email. The dietary restrictions live in a text thread. The
deposit confirmation is in a bank app. Six weeks later someone asks "what did we agree with the
Chen family about the vegan course count" and answering it costs a search across five systems, none
of which were designed to be searched together.

The cost is not the search. The cost is that the operator becomes the integration layer — the only
place where the whole picture exists.

## The approach

Conversational AI is a thin interface over a thick problem. It answers a question and then forgets,
which means the operator is still the one holding continuity.

RD-OS takes the opposite position. Operational information is organised into a **persistent
workspace** where business context remains visible, actionable and connected throughout the
lifecycle of the work. The context does not have to be re-established every time it is needed,
because it never went anywhere.

The design constraint throughout: **the system carries the burden, not the human.** Automation is
applied to recording, retrieval, aggregation and surfacing — the administrative overhead. Judgment
stays with the operator. The software never tells anyone what they must do; it makes the situation
legible enough that the decision is obvious.

## The Lead Tracker

The Lead Tracker is the capability that demonstrates this model most completely, because a sales
pipeline is where fragmentation hurts most and where the lifecycle is longest.

It combines, in one continuous workspace:

- **Customer information** — contact, company, source, ownership
- **Pipeline management** — a five-column board over a nine-stage lifecycle, with lost and archived
  work kept reachable rather than deleted
- **Dashboards** — eight live KPIs reconciled against the board itself
- **Workflow history** — a chronological timeline and an auto-recorded activity feed, both projected
  from the same underlying events so they can never disagree
- **Reporting** — six analytic views over the live book
- **Operational insight** — derived close probability, risk detection, missing-information
  detection and a recommended next action, all computed from the record's own contents

Everything a reviewer sees is derived from live data. There are no static mockups in the product
and none in the recording.

## On the demonstration data

The book of business shown is fictional and internally consistent by construction. A lead is never
idle longer than it has existed. Every stage a record sits in was arrived at by a transition that
appears in that record's own history. The pipeline contains genuine losses — one on price, one on
capacity — so the close rate is a real number rather than a flattering one.

No real client information appears anywhere in the demonstration.

## What this artifact is for

This is an employment demonstration. It is intended to let a reviewer evaluate product thinking,
workflow design, interface quality and operational reasoning.

It is deliberately not an architecture walkthrough. The implementation, the wider workspace and the
remaining capabilities are out of scope here.
